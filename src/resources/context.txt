EA 2025/2026, DEI, Instituto Superior Técnico, Universidade de Lisboa
1.	Project UoD 
ALMA&FUMO (Alma & Fumo – Portuguese Gourmet Restaurant Group) is a gourmet restaurant chain established in Portugal, operating in Lisbon, Porto, and Braga, with a “fine casual” positioning: contemporary Portuguese cuisine, strong emphasis on local DOP/IGP (EU quality labels) products, a curated wine list, and seasonal experiences (tasting menus, chef’s table, and themed events). The company combines in-restaurant service with digital channels (reserva-tions, premium take-away, and B2B catering/corporate events), aiming to grow while preserving a consistent brand identity, culinary quality, and operational excellence.

ALMA&FUMO’s (A&F) main challenge is scaling sustainably: ensuring consistent execution across units (food quality, service timing, staff training, and culture) while managing a demanding supply chain (artisan suppliers, seasonality, traceability, and waste reduction). In parallel, the group faces margin pressure and cost volatility (energy, ingredi-ents, labor), rising customer expectations (personalization, omnichannel journeys, transparency), and the need to modernize processes and systems (reservations, POS, inventory, procurement, HR, BI) to enable faster decision-making and controlled growth.
We next present information regarding A&F strategy, business, application and technology situation and main chal-lenges. Please note that the information was gathered from different sources, and it is also part of your job to ensure its consistency and relevance.

1.2 Motivation
A&F’s motivation is rooted in its mission to elevate Portuguese gastronomy through contemporary interpretation, uncompromising product quality, and a consistent premium experience across multiple locations and channels. The group aims to turn local provenance—ingredients, producers, and wine regions—into a scalable value proposition that preserves authenticity while enabling controlled growth.
Below is a breakdown of A&F’s core motivations:
•	Delivering a Consistent Premium Guest Experience
A&F aims to provide a reliably excellent dining experience across all units and channels by:
o	Standardizing service and kitchen execution without losing “craft” and hospitality warmth.
o	Offering high-touch moments (pairings, storytelling, celebrations) supported by efficient opera-tions.
o	Building trust through predictable quality, timing, and transparency (allergens, provenance).
•	Celebrating Portuguese Provenance and Culinary Identity
A&F is motivated to strengthen Portuguese culinary culture by:
o	Prioritizing high-quality local DOP/PGI products and seasonal ingredients.
o	Showcasing regional diversity through rotating menus and wine programs.
o	Giving visibility to producers and origin stories as part of the dining narrative.
•	Sustainable and Responsible Operations
A&F seeks to grow with responsibility and long-term viability by:
o	Reducing food waste through better forecasting, portioning, and inventory practices.
o	Improving energy and packaging sustainability, especially in take-away operations.
o	Ensuring ethical supplier relationships and transparent sourcing.
•	Operational Excellence and Margin Resilience
In a context of cost volatility, A&F is motivated to strengthen operational discipline by:
o	Improving procurement planning, stock accuracy, and yield management.
o	Optimizing labor scheduling and training to reduce turnover and maintain standards.
o	Managing profitability by channel (dine-in, events, take-away) and by menu engineering.
•	Growth Through Scalable Concepts and New Revenue Streams
A&F aims to expand while protecting brand integrity by:
o	Replicating a proven restaurant model into new locations with controlled variation.
o	Developing B2B and events (private dining, corporate catering) as structured offerings.
o	Enhancing premium take-away with quality-preserving processes and experience design.
•	Data-Driven Management and Continuous Improvement
A&F is motivated to modernize decision-making and coordination by:
o	Integrating reservations, POS, inventory, procurement, CRM/loyalty, and finance data.
o	Monitoring performance with clear KPIs (service time, satisfaction, waste, margin, utilization).
o	Enabling faster, evidence-based adjustments to menus, staffing, and supply planning.

Below, the strategies and actionable tactics related to each goal or driver are described.
1) Goal/Driver: Consistent Premium Guest Experience (multi-unit, multi-channel)
Strategy 1.1 — Standardize the essentials, personalize the moments
•	Tactics:
o	Define “non-negotiables” (plating standards, service timing targets, greeting/farewell rituals, com-plaint handling rules).
o	Create a Guest Experience Playbook shared across all units (FOH and BOH).
o	Implement reservation policies by segment (walk-ins vs. deposits for peak times; no-show rules; waitlist logic).
o	Introduce a guest profile concept (preferences, allergies, favorite tables, special occasions) to ena-ble consistent personalization.
Strategy 1.2 — Elevate service quality through training and measurement
•	Tactics:
o	Establish an internal A&F Academy (onboarding + continuous training; role-based certifications for hosts, servers, sommeliers).
o	Use “mystery guest” audits and structured checklists; close the loop with corrective actions.
o	Create a service KPI set per unit: average seating-to-order time, course pacing, complaint resolu-tion time, NPS/CSAT.
Strategy 1.3 — Protect the experience during peak load and disruptions
•	Tactics:
o	Implement capacity and pacing controls (table turn targets, kitchen throughput limits, buffer ta-bles).
o	Introduce contingency playbooks (supplier substitution rules, equipment failure response, staffing shortages).
o	Design omnichannel consistency (take-away packaging standards, pickup flow, delivery partner SLAs).

2) Goal/Driver: Provenance-Led Differentiation (Portuguese identity + seasonal innovation)
Strategy 2.1 — Make provenance explicit and scalable
•	Tactics:
o	Create a Provenance Catalog: ingredient origins, DOP/PGI attributes, producer profiles, seasonal availability calendar.
o	Establish menu rules: minimum % of local/DOP/PGI ingredients; rotating “region spotlight” items.
o	Standardize storytelling assets (menu descriptors, table talk tracks, wine pairing notes) in a reusa-ble content library.
Strategy 2.2 — Build long-term producer partnerships (quality + stability)
•	Tactics:
o	Segment suppliers (strategic producers vs. commodity distributors) and define partnership models per segment.
o	Co-plan seasonal volumes and agree substitution protocols; implement quality acceptance criteria at delivery.
o	Launch joint activations (producer dinners, wine-maker nights, “harvest menu” collaborations).
Strategy 2.3 — Innovate through a disciplined culinary pipeline
•	Tactics:
o	Define an R&D cadence (quarterly menu releases; monthly specials; test kitchen process).
o	Use structured feedback loops (dish performance, returns, guest sentiment, margin contribution).
o	Maintain a “signature core” menu to preserve identity, plus seasonal layers to keep novelty.

3) Goal/Driver: Sustainable and Responsible Operations (waste, footprint, credibility)
Strategy 3.1 — Treat waste reduction as an operational capability
•	Tactics:
o	Implement waste measurement by category (prep waste, spoilage, plate waste) and link to root causes.
o	Introduce forecasting-based prep planning; portion control standards; “use-first” inventory rules (FEFO).
o	Create waste-to-value practices (daily specials using surplus, staff meals, donation partnerships where feasible/legal).
Strategy 3.2 — Sustainability-by-design for take-away and logistics
•	Tactics:
o	Standardize packaging: recyclable/compostable options, heat retention, labeling for reheating and allergens.
o	Optimize pickup/delivery windows to reduce late deliveries and quality degradation.
o	Define sustainability KPIs: packaging mix, waste per cover, energy per service, supplier sustainabil-ity scoring.
Strategy 3.3 — Strengthen transparency and compliance readiness
•	Tactics:
o	Maintain traceability records (batch/lot where applicable), allergen controls, and HACCP evidence.
o	Create audit-ready documentation templates for suppliers, processes, and incident handling.
o	Publish “provenance & impact” communication (in-store and online) to reinforce credibility.

4) Goal/Driver: Operational Excellence and Margin Resilience (cost volatility, labor intensity)
Strategy 4.1 — Control unit economics through menu and procurement discipline
•	Tactics:
o	Implement menu engineering: contribution margin per dish, popularity vs. profitability, cross-sell pairing logic.
o	Standardize recipe costing and yield tracking; manage substitution impacts on cost and guest expe-rience.
o	Create purchasing frameworks: approved supplier lists, contract tiers, volume discounts, price monitoring.
Strategy 4.2 — Improve labor productivity without degrading hospitality
•	Tactics:
o	Adopt demand-based scheduling (reservations + historical data); define staffing baselines by service type.
o	Role clarity and micro-processes (station setup, handoffs between kitchen and FOH, bar batching).
o	Reduce churn via training pathways and predictable rostering; measure turnover cost and time-to-proficiency.
Strategy 4.3 — Strengthen operational control across units
•	Tactics:
o	Define standard operating procedures (SOPs) per process: opening/closing, stock counts, cash han-dling, incident response.
o	Implement a multi-unit performance cockpit: daily flash report (sales, covers, labor %, waste, com-plaints).
o	Create governance routines: weekly ops review, monthly finance review, quarterly strategy review.

5) Goal/Driver: Growth via scalable concepts and new revenue streams (events, B2B, take-away)
Strategy 5.1 — Expand through a repeatable “unit blueprint”
•	Tactics:
o	Define the A&F “unit template”: layout requirements, equipment standards, staffing model, train-ing plan.
o	Create a site selection scorecard (footfall, competition, rent-to-sales ratio, tourism mix).
o	Standardize the opening playbook: pre-opening training, soft opening, stabilization KPIs.
Strategy 5.2 — Professionalize events and B2B as products
•	Tactics:
o	Package events as defined offerings (private dining tiers, corporate menus, beverage packages, add-ons).
o	Establish an account management process: lead intake, proposal generation, contracting, invoicing, post-event review.
o	Define SLAs and risk rules: deposits, cancellation policies, dietary handling, capacity constraints.
Strategy 5.3 — Build premium take-away as a controlled channel
•	Tactics:
o	Design a “travel-ready” menu with explicit constraints (max holding time, reheatability, packaging fit).
o	Implement quality gates (final checklists, packaging labeling, handoff confirmation).
o	Track channel-specific profitability and satisfaction (on-time rate, refund rate, repeat purchase).

6) Goal/Driver: Data-Driven Management and Integrated Operations (systems + decision speed)
Strategy 6.1 — Establish a single operational truth across systems
•	Tactics:
o	Define master data ownership: menu items, recipes, suppliers, products, units, customer profiles.
o	Integrate core flows: reservations → POS → inventory → procurement → finance; unify reporting definitions.
o	Implement data quality controls: mandatory fields, validation rules, reconciliation routines.
Strategy 6.2 — Use analytics to drive continuous improvement
•	Tactics:
o	Create dashboards by persona: CEO/CFO (profitability), Ops (throughput/waste), Chef (dish per-formance), Marketing (segments/retention).
o	Implement experimentation loops: A/B testing for offers, reservation policies, menu placement, pairing suggestions.
o	Define early-warning indicators (stockout risk, no-show spikes, labor overrun, negative sentiment).
Strategy 6.3 — Protect trust through security and privacy
•	Tactics:
o	Apply GDPR-by-design for reservation/loyalty data: consent, minimization, retention schedules.
o	Enforce role-based access, audit logs, and incident response procedures.
o	Ensure PCI compliance and strong payment security controls for deposits and online transactions.

1.6 IT Layer
A&F (ALMA&FUMO) operates an all-cloud IT model with no owned data centers. 
Core business applications (Reservations, POS/KDS, Online Ordering, CRM/Loyalty, Inventory, Procurement, ERP/Accounting, Compliance/HACCP, BI, HR/LMS, Integration platform) are delivered primarily as SaaS, comple-mented by limited PaaS components for integration, data pipelines, and analytics. 
Restaurant locations connect to these cloud services through reliable broadband (with redundancy in critical sites), secure Wi-Fi segmentation for corporate and guest networks, and managed endpoints (POS terminals, kitchen dis-plays, tablets, back-office laptops). 
The technology priorities are availability during peak service, security and GDPR compliance, standardized connec-tivity across locations, and centralized monitoring to ensure operational continuity.

1.7 A&FChallenges
A&F (ALMA&FUMO) faces a set of business challenges driven by its premium positioning, multi-unit operations, and omnichannel strategy. The main challenges are described below, including their impact and potential solutions.
1.7.1 Business Challenges

1.7.1.1 Multi-Unit Consistency of Experience and Quality
Description: Ensuring the A&F experience (dish quality, service timing, hospitality warmth, storytelling, and service recovery) is consistently delivered across all locations and shifts.
Impact:
•	Execution variability reduces trust, repeat visits, and word-of-mouth referrals.
•	Online reputation becomes fragile: poor performance in one unit affects the entire brand.
•	Scaling becomes harder, as additional units amplify inconsistency.
Potential Solutions:
•	Operational playbooks and “non-negotiables” (service standards, kitchen execution, quality gates).
•	An internal training academy with role-based certification paths.
•	Regular QA audits (e.g., mystery guests) and KPI-driven management (service time, NPS/CSAT, complaints).

1.7.1.2 Premium Supply Chain Management (Seasonality, Quality Variability, Traceability)
Description: Dependence on premium local producers and seasonal ingredients increases the risk of shortages, vari-able quality, and logistical complexity, while requiring strong traceability and allergen control.
Impact:
•	Availability issues lead to menu instability and guest frustration.
•	Higher waste due to short shelf-life items and uncertain demand.
•	Increased compliance exposure (HACCP evidence, allergen accuracy, traceability during incidents).
Potential Solutions:
•	Supplier segmentation (strategic vs. commodity) and contracts with clear service/quality expectations.
•	Seasonal volume planning and governed substitution rules (pre-approved alternatives).
•	Strong receiving/acceptance controls and integrated procurement–inventory–menu/recipe management.

1.7.1.3 Margin Pressure and Cost Volatility (Energy, Ingredients, Labor)
Description: Gourmet operations are labor-intensive and highly sensitive to fluctuating input costs, while guests ex-pect premium quality without constant price increases.
Impact:
•	Profitability erosion by unit and by channel (dine-in vs take-away vs events).
•	Increased pricing pressure can damage value perception and demand.
•	Short-term decisions (cheaper inputs, understaffing) risk brand dilution.
Potential Solutions:
•	Menu engineering (contribution margin + popularity), recipe costing discipline, and yield management.
•	Procurement governance (price tracking, contract tiers, approved substitutes).
•	Demand-based staffing and productivity improvement without degrading hospitality.

1.7.1.4 Talent Availability and Capability Building (Service and Culinary Roles)
Description: Hiring and retaining skilled staff (kitchen, service, sommeliers) while maintaining culture and standards across units is a structural challenge in hospitality.
Impact:
•	High turnover increases training burden and consistency issues.
•	Skill gaps lead to slower service, more mistakes, and weaker guest experience.
•	Expansion plans can stall due to lack of ready teams for new openings.
Potential Solutions:
•	Structured onboarding and continuous training (A&F Academy), clear career pathways, role certification.
•	Workforce planning tied to growth roadmap and seasonal peaks.
•	Operational simplification and tooling that reduces friction and errors.

1.7.1.5 Omnichannel Growth Without Brand Dilution (Dine-in + Premium Take-away + Events)
Description: Expanding revenue streams introduces complexity and risks inconsistent quality across channels, espe-cially for take-away/delivery and large events.
Impact:
•	Brand perception suffers if off-premise quality or timing is inconsistent.
•	Operational overload and conflicting priorities during peak periods.
•	Increased refunds/complaints and lower repeat purchase in take-away.
Potential Solutions:
•	Channel-specific product governance (travel-ready menu rules, packaging standards, quality gates).
•	Standardized event packages with defined capacity, SLAs, deposits/cancellation policies.
•	Channel performance dashboards and explicit rules for prioritization during peak load.

1.7.1.6 Demand Variability and Capacity Management (No-Shows, Peaks, Seasonality)
Description: Restaurant revenue is highly dependent on balancing capacity utilization and pacing, while dealing with no-shows, uneven demand, and tourism-driven peaks.
Impact:
•	Lost revenue from no-shows and underutilization.
•	Overload leads to slower service, mistakes, and negative reviews.
•	Higher waste when forecasting is inaccurate.
Potential Solutions:
•	Reservation policy management (deposits for peak slots, waitlist optimization, pacing rules).
•	Forecasting using reservations + historical data to drive prep and staffing plans.
•	Continuous tuning of service throughput and table turn targets (without harming experience).

1.7.1.7 Reputation, Differentiation, and Competitive Pressure
Description: Competing in the premium dining space requires ongoing differentiation (provenance, innovation, expe-rience design) and proactive reputation management.
Impact:
•	Weak differentiation increases price sensitivity and reduces loyalty.
•	Negative reviews can rapidly reduce demand, especially among tourists and first-time guests.
•	Marketing spend rises if retention and referrals decline.
Potential Solutions:
•	Provenance-led positioning, seasonal innovation cadence, and curated experiences (wine dinners, chef’s ta-ble).
•	Structured review and feedback management with fast service recovery loops.
•	Loyalty/membership strategy focused on repeat visits and personalized engagement.

1.7.1.8 Governance and Scalability of the Operating Model
Description: As A&F grows, it must scale governance (decision rights, standards, data, and reporting) without be-coming bureaucratic or slow.
Impact:
•	Misalignment between units and central functions (menu, procurement, marketing).
•	Delayed decisions and inconsistent execution.
•	Limited visibility into performance and root causes across locations.
Potential Solutions:
•	Clear operating model: standardized processes + local autonomy boundaries.
•	Integrated reporting (unit scorecards) and governance routines (weekly ops review, monthly financial re-view).
•	Strong master data ownership and KPI definitions to ensure comparability across units.
